Analytical notes, supporting results, and response material for the eLife revision.

Goal: compare a covariance-dominated rule with the rate-dominated rule in a setting with multiple competing inhibitory-to-excitatory connections.

Throughout, we take \(h_e>0\), \(r_i>0\), and all kernel timescales positive. We assume \(M>0\) for both rules; for the covariance-dominated rule, this requires \(\tau_->\tau_+\). The inputs, inhibitory rate, fixed weight \(W_e\), and rule constants are held constant during the weight dynamics.

Three neuron motif

We consider a simple three-neuron motif, with one excitatory neuron and two inhibitory neurons, modeled as Hawkes processes (Figure 1). The excitatory neuron receives a constant input \(h_e\), and projects to one of the inhibitory neurons with a strong weight \(W_e\). The two inhibitory neurons project back to the excitatory neuron with plastic weights \(W_1\) and \(W_2\). All neurons have an exponential synaptic kernel with timescale \(\tau_{\text{syn}}\).

In the three-neuron system of Figure 1, the inhibitory rates \(r_i\) are kept constant by an adaptive input. The excitatory rate instead is given by: \[ r_e = h_e - W_1 \,r_i - W_2 \,r_i = h_e - (W_1 + W_2) \,r_i \tag{1}\]

The excitatory neuron projects to a single inhibitory neuron with a fixed weight \(W_e\), that we assume strong. Therefore inhibitory neuron 1 will have a strong correlation with the excitatory neuron, while inhibitory neuron 2 will be assumed as uncorrelated.

The two inhibitory-to-excitatory weights \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) are plastic, following identical symmetric plasticity rules. The average synaptic weight dynamics are given by: \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d\,W_1}{dt} &= \alpha_{\text{pre}} \,r_i + \alpha_{\text{post}} \,r_e + B \,r_i \,r_e - M^{10}\,W_1\,r_i + M^{01}\, W_e \, r_e \\ \frac{d\,W_2}{dt} &= \alpha_{\text{pre}} \,r_i + \alpha_{\text{post}} \,r_e + B \,r_i \,r_e - M^{10}\,W_2\,r_i \end{aligned} \tag{2}\] Where \(B\) is the area under the STDP curve, and the \(M\) terms represent the first-order motif contributions to plasticity (ignoring higher order contributions). Since here we only consider symmetric rules \(M^{10} = M^{01} := M\).

Let \(\eta>0\) be the learning constant multiplying the entire right-hand side of every weight equation. The dynamics and fixed-point derivations below display \(\eta=1\) for readability; the deviation matrices, eigenvalues, and relaxation times include \(\eta\) explicitly. Each rule may use its own \(\eta\), held constant during learning. It rescales time without changing the fixed point. The presynaptic and postsynaptic constants are calibrated parameters, rather than fixed nominal values.

In the Vogels-Spreckeler rule we have: \[ \alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{Vog}} = 0, \quad B^{\text{Vog}} = 1 \tag{3}\] The \(M^{\text{Vog}}\) term is: \[ M^{\text{Vog}} = \frac{1}{2\,\left(\tau_+ + \tau_{\text{syn}} \right)} \tag{4}\] And \(\alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{Vog}}\) is a free parameter set to ensure that the rate of the excitatory neuron is maintained at a target value \(r_e^{\text{(target)}}\).

In the covariance-dominated rule instead: \[ \quad B^{\text{cov}} = 0 ,\quad M^{\text{cov}} = \frac{1}{2\,\left(\tau_+ + \tau_{\text{syn}} \right)} - \frac{1}{2\,\left(\tau_- + \tau_{\text{syn}} \right)} \tag{5}\] And both \(\alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{cov}}\) and \(\alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{cov}}\) are free parameters that can be used to set the target rate. Here for simplicity we will set \(\alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{cov}} = 0\), but all our results hold in the general case (see detailed analytic notes).

For \(\tau_- \gg \tau_+\) and \(\tau_- \gg \tau_{\text{syn}}\), \(M^{\text{Vog}}\) and \(M^{\text{cov}}\) are similar. The main difference between the rules is therefore in the \(B^{\text{Vog}}=1\) and \(B^{\text{cov}}=0\) terms.

Sum and difference modes

Keeping the rule constants general, we define the sum and difference modes as

\[ W_s := W_1 + W_2, \qquad W_d := W_1 - W_2. \tag{6}\]

With this convention, \(W_d > 0\) means that \(W_1\) is stronger than \(W_2\). The original weights are recovered as

\[ W_1 = \frac{W_s + W_d}{2}, \qquad W_2 = \frac{W_s - W_d}{2}. \tag{7}\]

Using Equation 1 and the definition in Equation 6, the excitatory rate depends only on the sum mode:

\[ r_e = h_e - W_s\,r_i. \tag{8}\]

Adding and subtracting the two weight equations in Equation 2, with \(M^{10} = M^{01} = M\), gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d\,W_s}{dt} &= 2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i + 2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}\,r_e + 2\,B\,r_i\,r_e - M\,W_s\,r_i + M\,W_e\,r_e, \\ \frac{d\,W_d}{dt} &= -M\,W_d\,r_i + M\,W_e\,r_e. \end{aligned} \tag{9}\]

Substituting Equation 8 yields a closed system for the two modes:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d\,W_s}{dt} &= 2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i + \left(2\,\alpha_{\text{post}} + 2\,B\,r_i + M\,W_e\right) \left(h_e - W_s\,r_i\right) - M\,W_s\,r_i, \\ \frac{d\,W_d}{dt} &= -M\,W_d\,r_i + M\,W_e\,\left(h_e - W_s\,r_i\right). \end{aligned} \tag{10}\]

The shared rate-dependent plasticity terms cancel in the difference equation. The sum mode evolves independently of \(W_d\), while the difference mode is driven by the sum through \(r_e\) and by the additional \(W_e\) term in the \(W_1\) equation.

Fixed points of the modes and individual weights

To simplify the expressions, define

\[ c_1 := 2\,\alpha_{\text{post}} + 2\,B\,r_i + M\,W_e, \qquad c_2 := c_1 + M. \tag{11}\]

Using Equation 11, the sum equation in Equation 10 becomes

\[ \frac{d\,W_s}{dt} = 2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i + c_1\,h_e - r_i\,c_2\,W_s. \tag{12}\]

Substituting \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) from Equation 11, the fixed point simplifies to

\[ \begin{aligned} r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &= \frac{M\,h_e - 2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i} {M\,(1+W_e) + 2\,\alpha_{\text{post}} + 2\,B\,r_i}, \\ W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &= \frac{h_e-r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{r_i} = \frac{2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i + \left(2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+2\,B\,r_i+M\,W_e\right)\,h_e} {r_i\,\left[M\,(1+W_e)+2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+2\,B\,r_i\right]}, \\ W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &= \frac{W_e\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{r_i} = \frac{W_e\,\left(M\,h_e-2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i\right)} {r_i\,\left[M\,(1+W_e)+2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+2\,B\,r_i\right]}. \end{aligned} \tag{13}\]

Recovering the individual weights with Equation 7 gives

\[ \begin{aligned} W_1^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &= \frac{W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}+W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{2} = \frac{h_e+(W_e-1)\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{2\,r_i} \\ &= \frac{\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,(1-W_e)\,r_i + \left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i+M\,W_e\right)\,h_e} {r_i\,\left[M\,(1+W_e)+2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+2\,B\,r_i\right]}, \\ W_2^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &= \frac{W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}-W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{2} = \frac{h_e-(1+W_e)\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{2\,r_i} \\ &= \frac{\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,(1+W_e)\,r_i + \left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i\right)\,h_e} {r_i\,\left[M\,(1+W_e)+2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+2\,B\,r_i\right]}. \end{aligned} \tag{14}\]

These expressions assume a nonzero denominator; stability requires it to be positive, as shown below.

Thus, for \(W_e > 0\) and \(r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} > 0\), the difference is positive: \(W_1^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} > W_2^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\). This follows from the additional excitatory-to-inhibitory motif contribution to the first weight.

These are algebraic fixed points of the unconstrained equations. For nonnegative inhibitory weight magnitudes and excitatory rate, they must also satisfy

\[ r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} \ge 0, \qquad W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} \ge |W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}|. \tag{15}\]

For \(W_e \ge 0\), these conditions are equivalent to \(0 \le r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} \le h_e/(1+W_e)\). If they fail, the algebraic fixed point does not represent a state with nonnegative rates and weights; no weight clipping or boundary dynamics have been specified here.

Relative size of the difference and sum

For a stable fixed point with \(W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}>0\) and \(r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}>0\), the common denominator cancels in the ratio:

\[ \frac{W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}} = \frac{W_e\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{h_e-r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}} = \frac{W_e\,\left(M\,h_e-2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i\right)} {2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i + \left(2\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+2\,B\,r_i+M\,W_e\right)\,h_e}. \tag{16}\]

With strictly positive weights, the difference cannot be larger than the sum: \(W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}-W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}=2\,W_2^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}>0\). For \(W_e>0\), a physically admissible fixed point therefore has \(0<W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}/W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}<1\). A ratio close to one means \(W_2^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\) is small relative to \(W_1^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\); equality means \(W_2^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}=0\).

Setting the fixed-point rate equal to \(r_e^{\text{(target)}}\) requires

\[ \alpha_{\text{pre}} = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i}{r_i}\,r_e^{\text{(target)}} + \frac{M}{2\,r_i}\, \left[h_e-(1+W_e)\,r_e^{\text{(target)}}\right]. \tag{17}\]

TipMost important result here!

Given \(h_e\), \(r_i\), and \(W_e\), the stable weights depend on the plasticity parameters only through \(r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\). Therefore, if the parameters of each rule are chosen to produce the same target rate at a stable fixed point, the final inhibitory weights will also be the same across all possible rules considered here! The solution is rule-invariant, and the only difference between rules is in the dynamics of convergence to the fixed point!

Vogels-Sprekeler rule

With \(\alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{Vog}}=0\) and \(B^{\text{Vog}}=1\),

\[ r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} = \frac{M^{\text{Vog}}\,h_e-2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{Vog}}\,r_i} {M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+W_e)+2\,r_i}, \qquad \alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{Vog}} = -r_e^{\text{(target)}} + \frac{M^{\text{Vog}}}{2\,r_i}\, \left[h_e-(1+W_e)\,r_e^{\text{(target)}}\right]. \tag{18}\]

Covariance-based rule

With \(\alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{cov}}=0\) and \(B^{\text{cov}}=0\), assuming \(M^{\text{cov}}>0\) (thus \(\tau_->\tau_+\)),

\[ r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} = \frac{h_e}{1+W_e} - \frac{2\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{cov}}\,r_i} {M^{\text{cov}}\,(1+W_e)}, \qquad \alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{cov}} = \frac{M^{\text{cov}}}{2\,r_i}\, \left[h_e-(1+W_e)\,r_e^{\text{(target)}}\right]. \tag{19}\]

Convergence timescales of the sum and difference

The excitatory rate converges with the sum mode because \(r_e=h_e-r_i\,W_s\). Reaching the target rate does not imply that the weight difference has settled.

Writing \(\delta W_s := W_s-W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\) and \(\delta W_d := W_d-W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\), the deviations obey the exact linear system

\[ \frac{d}{dt} \begin{pmatrix}\delta W_s \\ \delta W_d\end{pmatrix} = \eta\, \begin{pmatrix} -r_i\,c_2 & 0 \\ -M\,W_e\,r_i & -M\,r_i \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}\delta W_s \\ \delta W_d\end{pmatrix}. \tag{20}\]

The triangular matrix has eigenvalues \(\lambda_s=-\eta\,r_i\,c_2\) and \(\lambda_d=-\eta\,r_i\,M\). Therefore both modes converge to the algebraic fixed point precisely when \(c_2>0\). Their exponential timescales are

\[ \tau_s = \frac{1}{\eta\,r_i\,c_2}, \qquad \tau_d = \frac{1}{\eta\,r_i\,M}. \tag{21}\]

The ratio used in the report is

\[ \frac{\tau_s}{\tau_d}=\frac{M}{M\,(1+W_e)+2\,(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i)}. \tag{22}\]

The sum relaxes independently as \(\delta W_s(t)=\delta W_s(0)\,e^{-t/\tau_s}\). The difference also receives a transient forcing from the sum, so its trajectory generally contains both timescales. Once the sum has settled, the difference relaxes toward \(W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\) on \(\tau_d\). When \(c_2=M\), the forcing can produce a \(t\,e^{-\eta\,M\,r_i\,t}\) transient. Special initial conditions can eliminate one of the exponential contributions.

The sum converges much faster than the difference when

\[ \frac{\tau_d}{\tau_s} = \frac{c_2}{M} = 1 + W_e + \frac{2\,\alpha_{\text{post}} + 2\,B\,r_i}{M} \gg 1, \qquad c_2>0. \tag{23}\]

Thus a strong \(W_e\), a sufficiently positive postsynaptic term, or a large positive rate-dependent term \(B\,r_i\) can produce a fast sum and a slow difference. The presynaptic constant \(\alpha_{\text{pre}}\) shifts the fixed point but does not change these eigenvalues. The inhibitory rate multiplies both decay rates and also affects their ratio through \(B\,r_i\).

Vogels-Sprekeler rule

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau_s^{\text{Vog}} &= \frac{1} {\eta^{\text{Vog}}\,r_i\,\left[M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+W_e)+2\,r_i\right]}, \\ \tau_d^{\text{Vog}} &= \frac{1}{\eta^{\text{Vog}}\,M^{\text{Vog}}\,r_i}.\\ \frac{\tau_s^{\text{Vog}}}{\tau_d^{\text{Vog}}} &= \frac{M^{\text{Vog}}} {M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+W_e)+2\,r_i}. \end{aligned} \tag{24}\]

For \(M^{\text{Vog}}\ll2\,r_i\), expanding gives

\[ \frac{\tau_s^{\text{Vog}}}{\tau_d^{\text{Vog}}} = \frac{M^{\text{Vog}}}{2\,r_i} - \frac{(1+W_e)\,(M^{\text{Vog}})^2}{4\,r_i^2} + \mathcal{O}\!\left((M^{\text{Vog}})^3\right). \tag{25}\]

Therefore \(\tau_s \ll \tau_d\) , the sum relaxes much faster than the difference in this regime.

Covariance-based rule

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau_s^{\text{cov}} &= \frac{1} {\eta^{\text{cov}}\,M^{\text{cov}}\,r_i\,(1+W_e)}, \\ \tau_d^{\text{cov}} &= \frac{1}{\eta^{\text{cov}}\,M^{\text{cov}}\,r_i}. \end{aligned} \tag{26}\]

\[ \frac{\tau_s^{\text{cov}}}{\tau_d^{\text{cov}}} = \frac{1}{1+W_e}. \tag{27}\]

This ratio is independent of \(M^{\text{cov}}\) and in our parameter range \(\mathcal{O}(1)\).

Matching the sum-mode timescales

For the same \(r_i\) and \(W_e\), imposing \(\tau_s^{\text{Vog}}=\tau_s^{\text{cov}}\) requires

\[ \frac{\eta^{\text{Vog}}}{\eta^{\text{cov}}} = \frac{M^{\text{cov}}\,(1+W_e)} {M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+W_e)+2\,r_i}. \tag{28}\]

With nonnegative \(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i\) and \(W_e>0\), \(c_2>M\), so \(\tau_s<\tau_d\): the difference mode is slower than the sum mode for both rules considered here. For the covariance rule with \(\alpha_{\text{post}}=0\), the modes have comparable timescales when \(W_e\) is of order one. A general nonzero postsynaptic constant is retained in Equation 21 and Equation 23.

For any prescribed common sum timescale \(T>0\), choose

\[ \eta^{\text{Vog}}=\frac{1}{T\,r_i\,[M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+W_e)+2\,r_i]}, \qquad \eta^{\text{cov}}=\frac{1}{T\,r_i\,M^{\text{cov}}\,(1+W_e)}. \tag{29}\]

The resulting difference timescales are \(\tau_d^{\text{Vog}}=T\,[1+W_e+2\,r_i/M^{\text{Vog}}]\) and \(\tau_d^{\text{cov}}=T\,(1+W_e)\). Matching the sum therefore leaves the Vogels difference mode slower. Learning rates rescale both modes together and do not change either rule’s timescale ratio or calibrated fixed point.

Generalization to N inhibitory neurons and K excitatory targets

We now consider one excitatory neuron and \(N\) inhibitory neurons, of which \(K\) receive an excitatory-to-inhibitory synapse of fixed weight \(W_e\) each (the \(w_e\) in the generalized setup). Here

\[ N,K\in\mathbb{N}, \qquad 1\le K<N. \tag{30}\]

Thus \(N\ge2\), and both targeted and untargeted inhibitory groups are nonempty. The three-neuron motif above is the special case \(N=2\), \(K=1\). The weight \(W_e\ge0\) is the weight of each excitatory synapse, so the total outgoing excitatory weight is \(K\,W_e\).

We retain the same modeling assumptions: all inhibitory rates are held at the common value \(r_i>0\) by adaptive inputs, all inhibitory-to-excitatory weights follow the same symmetric rule with \(M>0\), and only first-order motif contributions are included. We assume no additional inhibitory-to-inhibitory connections. This is a generalization of the averaged plasticity equations in Equation 2 within that approximation.

Two effective inhibitory weights

Under the assumed symmetry within each group, let \(W_c\) be the common inhibitory-to-excitatory weight of the \(K\) neurons receiving the excitatory connection, and \(W_u\) the common weight of the remaining \(N-K\) neurons. The subscripts \(c\) and \(u\) denote neurons with and without that direct excitatory connection. The two effective weights obey

\[ \begin{aligned} r_e&=h_e-r_i\,\left[K\,W_c+(N-K)\,W_u\right], \\ \frac{d\,W_c}{dt} &=\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i +\left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i+M\,W_e\right)\,r_e -M\,r_i\,W_c, \\ \frac{d\,W_u}{dt} &=\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i +\left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i\right)\,r_e -M\,r_i\,W_u. \end{aligned} \tag{31}\]

These equations preserve equal weights within each group. The weight \(W_c\) is a weight per synapse; the targeted group’s total inhibitory weight is \(K\,W_c\), and the untargeted group’s is \((N-K)\,W_u\).

Define the total weight and the difference between the two effective weights as

\[ W_s:=K\,W_c+(N-K)\,W_u, \qquad W_d:=W_c-W_u. \tag{32}\]

The inverse transformation is

\[ W_c=\frac{W_s+(N-K)\,W_d}{N}, \qquad W_u=\frac{W_s-K\,W_d}{N}. \tag{33}\]

Closed sum and difference equations

Multiplying the two equations in Equation 31 by their group sizes and adding, or subtracting them directly, yields

\[ \begin{aligned} r_e&=h_e-r_i\,W_s, \\ \frac{d\,W_s}{dt} &=N\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i +\left(N\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+N\,B\,r_i+K\,M\,W_e\right)\,r_e -M\,r_i\,W_s, \\ \frac{d\,W_d}{dt} &=-M\,r_i\,W_d+M\,W_e\,r_e. \end{aligned} \tag{34}\]

Only the sum equation acquires the population factors \(N\) and \(K\). The shared plasticity terms still cancel from the difference of effective weights. The excitatory contribution to that difference is \(M\,W_e\,r_e\) because each targeted neuron receives one synapse of weight \(W_e\).

Define the generalized coefficients

\[ \begin{aligned} c_1^{(N,K)}&:=N\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+N\,B\,r_i+K\,M\,W_e, \\ c_2^{(N,K)}&:=c_1^{(N,K)}+M =M\,(1+K\,W_e)+N\,\alpha_{\text{post}}+N\,B\,r_i. \end{aligned} \tag{35}\]

The closed equations are then

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d\,W_s}{dt} &=N\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i+c_1^{(N,K)}\,h_e -r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}\,W_s, \\ \frac{d\,W_d}{dt} &=-M\,r_i\,W_d+M\,W_e\,\left(h_e-r_i\,W_s\right). \end{aligned} \tag{36}\]

Fixed points and admissibility

For \(c_2^{(N,K)}\ne0\), the algebraic fixed point is

\[ \begin{aligned} r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &=\frac{M\,h_e-N\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i}{c_2^{(N,K)}}, \\ W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &=\frac{h_e-r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{r_i} =\frac{N\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i+c_1^{(N,K)}\,h_e} {r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}}, \\ W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &=\frac{W_e\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{r_i}, \\ W_c^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &=\frac{h_e+\left[(N-K)\,W_e-1\right]\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{N\,r_i} \\ &=\frac{\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,\left[1-(N-K)\,W_e\right]\,r_i +\left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i+M\,W_e\right)\,h_e} {r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}}, \\ W_u^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)} &=\frac{h_e-(1+K\,W_e)\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{N\,r_i} \\ &=\frac{\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,(1+K\,W_e)\,r_i +\left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i\right)\,h_e} {r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}}. \end{aligned} \tag{37}\]

For \(W_e>0\) and \(r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}>0\), each targeted synapse is stronger than each untargeted synapse.

For \(W_e\ge0\), nonnegative weights and excitatory rate at the fixed point are equivalent to

\[ 0\le r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\le\frac{h_e}{1+K\,W_e}. \tag{38}\]

Indeed, the upper bound is the condition \(W_u^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\ge0\), and \(W_c^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}=W_u^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}+W_e\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}/r_i\ge0\) then follows. Strictly positive rates and weights require strict inequalities. These are fixed-point conditions; they do not impose clipping or ensure that every unconstrained trajectory stays in the nonnegative domain.

Difference relative to the total weight

For \(W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}>0\) and \(r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}>0\),

\[ \frac{W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}} =\frac{W_e\,r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{h_e-r_e^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}. \tag{39}\]

Since \(W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}-K\,W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}=N\,W_u^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\), a useful normalized contrast is

\[ \rho_{N,K}:=\frac{K\,W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}} =\frac{K\,W_e\,\left(M\,h_e-N\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i\right)} {N\,\alpha_{\text{pre}}\,r_i+c_1^{(N,K)}\,h_e}. \tag{40}\]

For \(W_e>0\) and a fixed point with strictly positive rates and weights, \(0<\rho_{N,K}<1\), or equivalently \(0<W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}/W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}<1/K\). The boundary \(\rho_{N,K}=1\) means all untargeted weights vanish. For a stable algebraic fixed point, this boundary and its continuation to negative untargeted weights are determined by

\[ W_u^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}<0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha_{\text{pre}}\,(1+K\,W_e)\,r_i +\left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i\right)\,h_e<0. \tag{41}\]

The fraction of total inhibitory weight carried by the targeted group is

\[ \frac{K\,W_c^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}}{W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}} =\frac{K}{N}+\frac{N-K}{N}\,\rho_{N,K}. \tag{42}\]

Thus equal weights give a targeted share \(K/N\), while \(\rho_{N,K}\to1\) concentrates the total weight in the targeted group.

Convergence timescales of the two effective weights

Writing \(\delta W_s:=W_s-W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\) and \(\delta W_d:=W_d-W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\), the two-mode system satisfies

\[ \frac{d}{dt} \begin{pmatrix}\delta W_s\\\delta W_d\end{pmatrix} = \eta\, \begin{pmatrix} -r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}&0\\ -M\,W_e\,r_i&-M\,r_i \end{pmatrix} \,\begin{pmatrix}\delta W_s\\\delta W_d\end{pmatrix}. \tag{43}\]

Its two eigenvalues are

\[ \lambda_s=-\eta\,r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}, \qquad \lambda_d=-\eta\,M\,r_i. \tag{44}\]

Since \(M>0\) and \(r_i>0\), the unconstrained fixed point is asymptotically stable precisely when \(c_2^{(N,K)}>0\). The timescales and their ratio are

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau_s^{(N,K)}&=\frac{1}{\eta\,r_i\,c_2^{(N,K)}}, &\tau_d^{(N,K)}&=\frac{1}{\eta\,M\,r_i}, \\ \frac{\tau_d^{(N,K)}}{\tau_s^{(N,K)}} &=1+K\,W_e+\frac{N\,\left(\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i\right)}{M}. \end{aligned} \tag{45}\]

The sum is much faster when this ratio is much larger than one. Increasing \(K\) at fixed weight per excitatory synapse strengthens the total feedback contribution \(K\,W_e\); increasing \(N\) scales the shared postsynaptic and rate-dependent terms. Neither changes the intrinsic difference timescale when \(M\) and \(r_i\) are fixed. The difference is forced by the sum and generally contains both timescales. If \(c_2^{(N,K)}=M\), this forcing can produce a \(t\,e^{-\eta\,M\,r_i\,t}\) transient, as in the three-neuron case.

Target-rate calibration and rule comparison for general N and K

Setting Equation 37 equal to the target rate gives

\[ \alpha_{\text{pre}} =-\frac{\alpha_{\text{post}}+B\,r_i}{r_i}\,r_e^{\text{(target)}} +\frac{M}{N\,r_i}\,[h_e-(1+K\,W_e)\,r_e^{\text{(target)}}]. \tag{46}\]

With this calibration, the final weights in Equation 37 depend on the rule only through the common target rate. Strictly positive weights and rate require \(0<r_e^{\text{(target)}}<h_e/(1+K\,W_e)\).

For the report’s choices \(\alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{Vog}}=\alpha_{\text{post}}^{\text{cov}}=0\), \(B^{\text{Vog}}=1\), and \(B^{\text{cov}}=0\), the calibrated constants are

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{Vog}} &=-r_e^{\text{(target)}}+\frac{M^{\text{Vog}}}{N\,r_i}\,[h_e-(1+K\,W_e)\,r_e^{\text{(target)}}],\\ \alpha_{\text{pre}}^{\text{cov}} &=\frac{M^{\text{cov}}}{N\,r_i}\,[h_e-(1+K\,W_e)\,r_e^{\text{(target)}}]. \end{aligned} \tag{47}\]

Including the rule-specific learning rates, the timescales are

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau_s^{(N,K),\text{Vog}}&=\frac{1}{\eta^{\text{Vog}}\,r_i\,[M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+K\,W_e)+N\,r_i]}, &\tau_d^{(N,K),\text{Vog}}&=\frac{1}{\eta^{\text{Vog}}\,M^{\text{Vog}}\,r_i},\\ \tau_s^{(N,K),\text{cov}}&=\frac{1}{\eta^{\text{cov}}\,M^{\text{cov}}\,r_i\,(1+K\,W_e)}, &\tau_d^{(N,K),\text{cov}}&=\frac{1}{\eta^{\text{cov}}\,M^{\text{cov}}\,r_i}. \end{aligned} \tag{48}\]

The difference-to-sum timescale ratios are \(1+K\,W_e+N\,r_i/M^{\text{Vog}}\) and \(1+K\,W_e\), respectively. Matching the sum timescales requires

\[ \frac{\eta^{\text{Vog}}}{\eta^{\text{cov}}} =\frac{M^{\text{cov}}\,(1+K\,W_e)}{M^{\text{Vog}}\,(1+K\,W_e)+N\,r_i}. \tag{49}\]

For general postsynaptic constants, replace the numerator and denominator by each rule’s \(c_2^{(N,K)}\) from Equation 35. With nonnegative shared postsynaptic and rate-dependent contributions, the sum is at least as fast as the difference.

Recovery of the three-neuron case

Setting \(N=2\) and \(K=1\) gives \(W_c=W_1\), \(W_u=W_2\), \(W_s=W_1+W_2\), and \(W_d=W_1-W_2\). The generalized coefficients become \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) from Equation 11, and Equation 34, Equation 37, and Equation 45 reduce to the previous dynamics, fixed points, and timescales. The normalized contrast becomes \(\rho_{2,1}=W_d^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}/W_s^{\left( t \rightarrow \infty \right)}\).

Conclusions

At a common calibrated target rate, the stable fixed point is rule-invariant. In the small-motif regime, the Vogels sum mode relaxes much faster than its difference mode. For the covariance rule with zero postsynaptic constant and \(W_e\) of order one, the two timescales are comparable. Matching the sum timescales therefore gives faster weight separation under the covariance rule, as illustrated by the supplied simulations. Both rules still have a difference mode slower than their sum mode.

The report raises the possibility that finite simulation durations can mask slow weight adaptation in existing work. This is an interpretation to investigate; establishing it for another model requires that model’s parameters and convergence analysis.
